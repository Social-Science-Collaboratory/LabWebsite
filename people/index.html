Nicholas Coles
Director

Dr. Nicholas Coles is the director of the Social Science Collaboratory. Before joining the University of Florida, he received his PhD from the University of Tennessee, completed a postdoctoral fellowship at Harvard University, and served as a research scientist at the Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI. His work has been published in outlets like Nature, Nature Human Behaviour, and Science – receiving coverage from outlets like Science Magazine, FiveThirtyEight, Nature News, and the Guardian. These contributions have been recognized via several awards, including: the Einstein Foundation Award for Promoting Quality in Research, the CORES Open Science Innovator Award, and three fellowhips from the U.S National Science Foundation. In his free time, Nicholas enjoys joking about other things that don’t exist.

Abhay Alaukik
Post-Doc Researcher

Abhay is a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Florida working at the intersection of formal social and cognitive modeling. He received his MS and PhD in Psychology from the University of Florida. Broadly, he implements mathematical models and computational social science approaches to enhance theorization across topics. Some of his current projects include modeling (a) big team science trust dynamics, (b) agentic AI-human interactions, (c) goal-directed information search strategies, and (d) social group evaluations/associations.

In his free time, he enjoys visiting national parks and logging reviews on Letterboxd.

Cindy-Rui Jin
Graduate Student

Cindy is a fourth-year Ph.D. student in social psychology at the University of Florida. She received her BSocSc and Mphil in cognitive psychology from the University of Hong Kong. Her research follows the movement of minds and people: using large-scale bibliometric datasets to uncover how academic brain drain unfolds when societies are disrupted, and how those shifts redirect scholars’ careers across the globe. She also explores everyday migration inside countries, showing how routine residential moves can quietly reshape local culture and influence people’s attitudes and behavior. Away from data and maps, Cindy unwinds by cooking, gaming, and relaxing with her cat, Toaster.
